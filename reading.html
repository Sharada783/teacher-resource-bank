Disciplinary Literacy

Help pupils learn how experts read, write, think and communicate within each subject.

Core concept

What Is Disciplinary Literacy?

Disciplinary literacy recognises that every subject has its own language, conventions and ways of reading, writing, speaking and thinking.

It involves teaching pupils to

  • Read texts in ways that suit the subject
  • Recognise subject-specific text structures
  • Use technical vocabulary accurately
  • Construct explanations and arguments appropriately
  • Communicate like an expert in the discipline

Subject-specific reading

Reading Like an Expert

Expert readers use different approaches depending on the discipline. A scientist, historian and mathematician may each attend to different features of a text.

Examples

  • Scientists examine evidence, reliability, technical language and relationships between text and diagrams.
  • Historians consider provenance, context, perspective and competing interpretations.
  • Mathematicians move between prose, symbols, equations, graphs and representations.
  • Geographers combine written text with maps, data, images and spatial evidence.

Technical language

Disciplinary Vocabulary

Pupils need more than definitions. They need repeated opportunities to hear, read, discuss, retrieve and apply subject-specific vocabulary in meaningful contexts.

Effective vocabulary teaching includes

  • Explicit explanation of meaning
  • Examples and non-examples
  • Morphology, roots, prefixes and suffixes
  • Connections between related words
  • Repeated retrieval and application
  • Use in speaking and extended writing

Cross-curricular language

Tier 2 Vocabulary

Tier 2 words are ambitious, high-utility words that appear across different subjects and types of text. They are often important for reading comprehension, discussion and academic writing.

Examples

  • Analyse
  • Interpret
  • Significant
  • Contrast
  • Justify
  • Consequence

Select words that

  • Appear across several subjects or contexts
  • Are important for understanding demanding texts
  • Help pupils express complex ideas precisely
  • Are unlikely to be learned through everyday conversation

Subject-specific language

Tier 3 Vocabulary

Tier 3 words are specialist terms associated with a particular subject, topic or discipline. Pupils need to understand both their precise definitions and how experts use them.

Examples

  • Science: photosynthesis, isotope, homeostasis
  • Mathematics: coefficient, congruent, gradient
  • History: suffrage, imperialism, chronology
  • Geography: urbanisation, erosion, sustainability

Teach more than a definition

  • Show the word in a meaningful subject context
  • Use diagrams, examples and non-examples
  • Explain related words and word families
  • Contrast the term with easily confused concepts
  • Require accurate use in explanations and arguments

Explicit instruction

Vocabulary Instruction

Effective vocabulary teaching combines clear explanation with repeated encounters, retrieval, discussion and purposeful application. Introducing a definition is only the beginning.

A useful teaching sequence

  1. Select a small number of high-value words.
  2. Explain each word clearly in context.
  3. Explore morphology, roots and related words.
  4. Use examples, non-examples and visual representations.
  5. Provide opportunities to speak and write using the word.
  6. Retrieve and revisit the word over time.

Ways to deepen word knowledge

  • Compare related and contrasting terms
  • Sort examples and non-examples
  • Explore prefixes, suffixes and roots
  • Use words in several different contexts
  • Ask pupils to explain connections between words
