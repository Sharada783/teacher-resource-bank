Using Research Critically

Questions and tools for deciding whether a finding is relevant, trustworthy and appropriate for your context.

Critical reading

Questions to Ask About Research

Who conducted and funded the research?

What question was the study trying to answer?

Who participated, and how large was the sample?

Was there a comparison or control group?

How were outcomes measured?

How large and meaningful was the effect?

What limitations did the authors identify?

How similar is the research context to mine?

Has the finding been supported by other studies?

From evidence to practice

Applying Research Thoughtfully

Evidence should inform professional judgement rather than replace it. A promising approach still needs careful planning, implementation and evaluation.

A practical sequence

  1. Identify a specific pupil or curriculum need.
  2. Review the most relevant evidence.
  3. Consider fit with the local context.
  4. Define what faithful implementation involves.
  5. Introduce the approach carefully.
  6. Monitor implementation and pupil outcomes.
  7. Adapt, continue or stop according to evidence.
