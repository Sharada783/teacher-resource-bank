Practical Science and Scientific Enquiry

Resources for planning purposeful practical work, developing enquiry skills and helping pupils reason from evidence.

Purposeful practical work

Planning Effective Practical Science

Practical work should have a clear learning purpose. Pupils need opportunities to connect observations and measurements to scientific ideas, rather than simply follow a method.

Planning questions

What scientific idea should the practical reveal?

Which decisions can pupils make independently?

What evidence will pupils collect?

How will observations connect to an explanation?

Which limitations or uncertainties should be considered?

Try this

Before giving pupils the full method, ask them to identify the independent, dependent and control variables and justify how each should be managed.

Working scientifically

Scientific Enquiry and Evaluation

Scientific enquiry involves asking testable questions, designing methods, interpreting evidence and evaluating the quality and limitations of conclusions.

Useful prompts

What question can be investigated?

Which evidence would answer the question?

How could the measurements be made more reliable?

What pattern is visible in the data?

Does the evidence support the conclusion?

What alternative explanation might exist?

Increase challenge by asking pupils to

  • Design or adapt the method
  • Justify the range and intervals selected
  • Identify anomalous data
  • Compare precision, accuracy and reliability
  • Evaluate the strength of a conclusion

Chemical thinking

Developing Deeper Chemistry Understanding

Chemistry becomes more challenging when pupils connect observations at the macroscopic level with particle models, symbolic equations and quantitative reasoning.

Useful prompts

What is happening at the particle level?

How does the equation represent the observed change?

Which evidence supports the proposed reaction?

What are the limitations of this model?

How would changing the conditions affect the outcome?

Try this

Ask pupils to explain the same chemical event using three representations: an observation, a particle diagram and a symbolic equation.

Physics thinking

Models, Explanations and Mathematics

High-quality physics teaching helps pupils connect physical phenomena, conceptual models, diagrams, graphs and mathematical relationships.

Useful prompts

What assumptions does this model make?

How does the graph represent the physical process?

What does the gradient or area mean here?

Which evidence would distinguish between two explanations?

Where does the model stop being useful?

Try this

Give pupils a graph, diagram and equation representing the same physical situation. Ask them to explain the connections between all three.

Diagnostic questioning

Reveal Thinking Before Reteaching

Diagnostic questions should help teachers discover how pupils are thinking, not merely whether their final answer is correct.

Useful approaches

  • Multiple-choice questions with plausible distractors
  • Prediction before observation
  • Confidence ratings
  • Drawing and annotating models
  • Explaining why an incorrect answer is tempting

Useful prompts

Why did you choose that answer?

What would someone with a different view say?

Which evidence could change your mind?

How does your model explain the observation?
