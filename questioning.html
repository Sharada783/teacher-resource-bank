Frameworks

Use these models to plan questions that increase the depth and complexity of pupils' thinking.

Essential framework

Bloom's Taxonomy

A framework for planning learning that moves pupils from remembering and understanding towards applying, analysing, evaluating and creating.

Useful for

  • Planning higher-order questions
  • Increasing the cognitive challenge of tasks
  • Writing learning objectives
  • Designing assessments and classroom activities

The six cognitive processes

  1. Remember: retrieve relevant knowledge
  2. Understand: explain ideas and concepts
  3. Apply: use knowledge in a situation
  4. Analyse: examine relationships and patterns
  5. Evaluate: make and justify judgements
  6. Create: produce or design something new

Example question stems

Remember: What can you recall about...?

Understand: How would you explain...?

Apply: How could you use this idea to...?

Analyse: What similarities, differences or patterns do you notice?

Evaluate: Which approach is most effective, and why?

Create: How could you design, improve or produce...?

Essential framework

Webb's Depth of Knowledge (DOK)

Developed by Norman Webb, the Depth of Knowledge framework measures the cognitive complexity of a task rather than its difficulty. It helps teachers design activities that require increasingly sophisticated thinking.

Useful for

  • Planning challenging learning activities
  • Improving higher-order questioning
  • Designing assessments
  • Increasing depth rather than workload

The four DOK levels

  1. Level 1: Recall and Reproduction
  2. Level 2: Skills and Concepts
  3. Level 3: Strategic Thinking
  4. Level 4: Extended Thinking

Essential framework

SOLO Taxonomy

SOLO (Structure of the Observed Learning Outcome) helps teachers understand how pupils' thinking develops from simple recall towards increasingly connected and abstract understanding.

Useful for

  • Assessment for learning
  • Success criteria
  • Progression in thinking
  • Self and peer assessment

The five SOLO levels

  1. Pre-structural
  2. Uni-structural
  3. Multi-structural
  4. Relational
  5. Extended Abstract

Essential strategy

Socratic Questioning

Socratic questioning encourages pupils to explain, justify, challenge and refine their thinking through carefully sequenced dialogue rather than simple recall questions.

Useful for

  • Class discussion
  • Critical thinking
  • Oracy
  • Developing reasoning

Common question types

  1. Clarification
  2. Probing assumptions
  3. Examining evidence
  4. Exploring viewpoints
  5. Considering implications
  6. Reflecting on the question
