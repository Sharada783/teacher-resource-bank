Metacognition and Self-Regulation

Help pupils plan, monitor and evaluate their own learning.

Before the task

Planning Learning

Before beginning a task, pupils identify the goal, activate relevant prior knowledge and choose a suitable strategy. Planning helps them approach learning deliberately rather than immediately starting without considering how to proceed.

Teach pupils to consider

  • What the task is asking them to achieve
  • What they already know that may help
  • Which strategy, method or resource to use
  • What difficulties they might encounter
  • How they will recognise a successful outcome

Planning prompts

What is the goal of this task?

What relevant knowledge can you retrieve?

Which strategy will you use, and why?

What might be difficult?

What will a strong response include?

Try this

Before independent work, give pupils one minute to write a short plan. Ask them to name the strategy they intend to use and explain why it is appropriate.

During the task

Monitoring Progress

While working, pupils check whether they understand the task, whether their chosen strategy is working and whether they need to change their approach.

Teach pupils to notice

  • Whether they are making progress towards the goal
  • Where confusion or errors are occurring
  • Whether their strategy remains appropriate
  • When to persist and when to change approach
  • Which type of support would help without removing the thinking

Monitoring prompts

Does your approach appear to be working?

What have you understood so far?

Where have you become stuck?

What evidence shows that you are making progress?

Should you continue, adapt or try another strategy?

Try this

Build a planned pause into an extended task. Ask pupils to mark their current progress, identify one difficulty and decide whether they need to adjust their strategy.

After the task

Evaluating Learning

After completing a task, pupils judge the quality of the outcome and reflect on the effectiveness of the process they used. Evaluation should inform what they do in a future task.

Teach pupils to evaluate

  • How well the final outcome met the goal
  • Which strategy was most useful
  • Which difficulties remained unresolved
  • What feedback or evidence reveals
  • What they would retain or change next time

Evaluation prompts

How successful was your approach?

Which part of your work is strongest, and why?

What mistake or difficulty taught you something useful?

What would you change if you repeated the task?

When might you use this strategy again?

Try this

Replace a general question such as “How did it go?” with three focused prompts: What worked? What did not work? What will you do differently next time?

Scaffolding and Gradual Release

Provide enough support for success, then remove it deliberately as pupils become more secure.

Gradual Release of Responsibility

Model, Guide, Release

Pupils become independent through carefully planned stages. Teachers first model expert thinking, then provide guided practice before gradually handing responsibility to pupils.

The gradual release model

  • I do – teacher modelling
  • We do – guided practice
  • You do together – collaborative practice
  • You do independently – independent application

Planning questions

Which parts should I model explicitly?

Where will pupils need guided practice?

When can responsibility be released safely?

Try this

Before independent work, think aloud while modelling your own reasoning so pupils can hear the decisions an expert makes.

Scaffolding

Fading Scaffolds

Effective scaffolds are temporary. As pupils become more secure, prompts and support should be gradually withdrawn to encourage independence.

Examples of scaffolds

  • Worked examples
  • Sentence starters
  • Writing frames
  • Prompt sheets
  • Checklists

Questions to ask

Which scaffold is no longer needed?

Can pupils now complete this step independently?

How can I reduce support without increasing confusion?

Try this

Remove one scaffold each lesson rather than removing every support at once.

Teacher Prompting

Prompt Before Telling

Rather than immediately providing the answer, use prompts, questions and cues that encourage pupils to retrieve prior knowledge and solve the problem themselves.

Prompting strategies

  • Ask pupils what they already know
  • Direct them to previous learning
  • Offer a cue instead of the solution
  • Break the task into manageable steps
  • Encourage pupils to explain their thinking aloud

Useful prompts

What have you already tried?

Where have you seen something similar before?

What information do you already have?

What would be a sensible next step?

Try this

Count silently to five before responding to a request for help. Often pupils solve the problem themselves when given thinking time.

Independent Learning Routines

Establish predictable routines that help pupils manage their own thinking, resources and next steps.

Before Asking for Help

Teach pupils a short sequence of actions to try before seeking direct teacher assistance.

Self-Checking

Give pupils criteria, examples and checking routines so they can identify and correct errors.

Choice and Decision-Making

Build structured opportunities for pupils to choose methods, resources, representations or task pathways.

Reflection and Retrieval

Use regular retrieval and reflection to help pupils recognise what they know and what they need to revisit.
